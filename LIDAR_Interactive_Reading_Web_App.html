Reading summary by paragraph

Opening: Technology can help researchers locate evidence of civilizations that disappeared.

1. The Maya were a major civilization in the Americas before European arrival. Their civilization reached its peak from AD 250 to AD 900. They built impressive stone structures, grew crops such as corn, developed mathematics and writing, and created a solar calendar. Their decline began around AD 900, but the exact cause is uncertain; possibilities mentioned include war, volcanic activity, and drought.

2. Researchers are learning more about the Maya through investigations in Mexico and Guatemala. LIDAR is the technology that has made many of these discoveries possible.

3. LIDAR means Light Detection and Ranging. It uses lasers to create three-dimensional images. It can be used for mapping, weather forecasting, space exploration, and self-driving cars. For archaeology, LIDAR equipment can be carried by aircraft; laser signals reveal patterns of natural and human-made features, even when vegetation hides them.

4. At Aguada Fénix in Mexico, LIDAR revealed the full size of a very large earth platform with buildings on top. The structure is considered the oldest and largest known structure in the Mayan region and is larger than Egypt's biggest pyramid. Researchers estimate that it required thousands of people and several years to construct and may have been used for ceremonies. Roads leading to it support that interpretation.

5. The absence of nearby houses suggests that people may not have lived at the site. The structure may represent a transition toward larger, more permanent settlements. Its size also indicates cooperation among different groups. Patterns in different soil colors may have represented contributions from those groups.

6. In Guatemala, the PACUNAM LIDAR Initiative surveyed about 2,100 square kilometers from the air. It revealed ruins showing that the civilization was more complex and advanced than researchers had previously believed.

7. LIDAR changed what researchers knew about Tikal. A feature once thought to be a small hill was actually a building that may have been ceremonial and may have contained a royal tomb. Researchers also discovered a large fortress between Tikal and nearby El Zotz. The fortress included watchtowers, moats, and high walls, suggesting concern about attacks.

8. LIDAR research has economic as well as academic value. In Guatemala, ancient ruins support tourism, and newly discovered sites can create new attractions. LIDAR can also help build cultural databases and document damage caused by vandalism or theft.

9. LIDAR has limits. It can reveal features below vegetation and can help researchers locate tunnels or buried ruins, but it does not eliminate the need for physical work. It makes investigations faster and more efficient by providing accurate maps. Much remains unknown about the Maya, so LIDAR is expected to remain useful for future research.
